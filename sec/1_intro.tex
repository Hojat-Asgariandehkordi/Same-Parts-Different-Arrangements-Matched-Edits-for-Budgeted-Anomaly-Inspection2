\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A pushpin tray in which one pin has slid into its neighbor's compartment contains only normal-looking parts. It is faulty because of where they are: one compartment is empty and another holds two pins (\cref{fig:teaser}). Logical anomaly detectors are built for such cases \cite{bergmann2022,fucka2025,liu2023,kim2024psad,hsieh2024csad}. They are expected not only to flag the tray but also to show an inspector which compartments to open, and MVTec LOCO scores this localization with the saturated per-region overlap (sPRO) \cite{bergmann2022}.

Most of these detectors learn to localize from synthetic defects \cite{li2021cutpaste,zavrtanik2021draem,schluter2022nsa,zhao2024,fucka2025}. Each synthetic defect comes with a pixel label, and that label defines what the detector is rewarded for marking. Its appearance is studied extensively; its spatial extent much less so. SALAD, a state-of-the-art composition-based detector \cite{fucka2025}, labels an inserted part over every instance of its class, because an extra screw in an unordered bag cannot be told apart from the original screws. Whether such a choice helps or hurts is hard to see on natural test sets, where faults are rare, every scene differs and nothing is held fixed. Threshold-free scores such as AUROC and sPRO also do not say how much work a map creates at a fixed operating point, or why.

We make the question measurable. \emph{Matched rearrangements} edit real photographs so that one factor changes at a time (\cref{fig:protocol}). The same intact pin moves either within its compartment, a valid sham, or into an occupied neighbor, a fault; a vacancy-only control removes it. Every operating point is fixed on development trays before test maps are read, and the cost of an alarm is counted in compartments opened. We then intervene on the label itself. We retrain SALAD's discriminative head twice on bit-identical inputs, changing only whether an insertion is labelled over its whole class (\emph{class-wide}, as released) or over the pasted region (\emph{footprint}). A short analysis predicts what each head should learn (\cref{prop:optimal}).

The evidence is consistent. The released detector flags all \relFaults{} faulty trays and ranks both affected compartments first or second on most of them, yet its calibrated threshold opens \relThrW{} of 13 intact compartments, because inserting a pin raises \relAnyFaultX\% of the untouched compartments. Footprint labels remove this spread (to \fpSpread\% in a continuation of the released head, and to \scrSpreadFP\% when SALAD is retrained from scratch). On the 91 real logical anomalies of MVTec LOCO pushpins, the from-scratch footprint heads reach an AU-sPRO of \scrSproFP\%, against \scrSproCW\% for their class-wide twins. At operating points fixed on development data they find \scrOpR\% of the defective compartments while opening \scrOpW{} intact compartments per faulty tray; the released detector's best policy finds \opRelListR\% and opens \opRelListW. Breakfast boxes improve as well. In screw bags, where the benchmark itself treats any of several identical parts as the extra one, footprint labels do not help, exactly as the analysis predicts.

\Cref{fig:teaser} shows one test tray. All three heads alarm and rank the two faulty compartments first and second. At the cutoff that reaches 95\% target recall on the development trays, the released head opens all \toyRel{} intact compartments, the retrained class-wide head opens \toyCW{} and the footprint head opens \toyFP. The released map changes by at least 0.5 in \toySpreadRel{} of the 13 untouched compartments when the pin is inserted, and the footprint map in \toySpreadFP.

Our paper offers the following contributions. First, we introduce matched rearrangements with valid shams and vacancy-only controls on real photographs, together with an inspection protocol that fixes thresholds, list lengths and scene cutoffs on development data and reports the compartments an inspector must open. Second, we show that SALAD's class-wide insertion label makes its map spread to every intact part of the inserted class. We explain why with an optimality argument and confirm it causally by retraining on identical inputs, both as continuations in five categories and from scratch with SALAD's full recipe in a registered replication. Third, we show that labelling the pasted footprint, which every synthetic edit already provides, more than doubles localization of real pushpin defects and improves breakfast boxes. At fixed operating points it finds more defective compartments while opening fewer intact ones than the released detector's best policy. The class-wide label remains preferable when the task itself treats instances as interchangeable. Fourth, we show that valid shams overturn conclusions that fault-only evaluation draws about appearance detectors and about SALAD's full system.
