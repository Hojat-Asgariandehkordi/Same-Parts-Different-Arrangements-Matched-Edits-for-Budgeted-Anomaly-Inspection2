\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Datasets}
All data come from MVTec LOCO \cite{bergmann2022}. For matched rearrangements we use every good pushpin photograph of the test split (138) and of the validation split (69); in each, the released part map resolves one intact pin per compartment, so every photograph yields all four edits. The validation trays form the development set on which every cutoff is fixed. For real defects we use the official test splits with their pixel annotations: pushpins (91 logical anomalies, 138 good images), breakfast boxes (83 and 102), juice bottles (142 and 94), splicing connectors (108 and 119) and screw bags (137 and 122). Pushpin faults are tied to compartments, breakfast-box faults to the content of a compartment, juice-bottle faults to single labels and the liquid, and screw-bag faults to the number of interchangeable parts in an unordered bag.

\subsection{Experimental settings}
We use SALAD's released weights and pinned code \cite{fucka2025}. To change only the insertion label, we continue training the released discriminative U-Net on SALAD's own edit stream with the released reconstruction autoencoder frozen. Training runs 1{,}600 steps of one possibly edited map plus its clean copy, with each of the four pinned edit families applied with total probability 1/2, SALAD's loss of five times the sigmoid focal loss plus L1, and Adam at $10^{-5}$ with a tenfold decay at 95\%. For each seed, one process copies the released weights into a class-wide and a footprint arm and feeds both the same prefetched stream of maps, donors and edits, so their inputs are identical bit for bit. No rule-specific edit is used. We train two seeds per category. For a registered replication we also train the whole composition branch, autoencoder and U-Net, from random initialization with SALAD's published recipe: 70{,}000 steps, multiclass focal plus Dice reconstruction loss, and the same discriminative loss, edit families and optimizer. The two arms again share initialization and edit stream within each seed. The re-segmenting U-Net is trained on official good training photographs only.

\subsection{Baselines}
The released SALAD composition head is the main baseline. Because our retraining is a continuation, its class-wide arm is the matched control for the footprint arm; differences between the two isolate the label. On the released map we also evaluate list and relative queues, which can partly compensate for poor calibration without retraining. EfficientAD with SALAD's released appearance weights \cite{batzner2024} and our implementation of PatchCore \cite{roth2022} (WideResNet-50, 1\% coreset) read the photographs instead of part maps; they test what matched shams reveal about maps that see the edit. A detector that applies the published one-pin-per-compartment rule to the re-segmented part map serves as a reference: it needs knowledge of the rule that a learned detector does not have. We do not retrain other part-based detectors \cite{liu2023,kim2024psad,hsieh2024csad}, because they do not learn localization from labelled synthetic insertions, so the intervention we study does not apply to them.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}
On matched trays we report alarms, target recall $R$ and wasted inspections $W$ (\cref{eq:cost}), and the \emph{spread}: the fraction of the 13 untouched compartments in which the map changes by at least 0.5 when the edit is applied. On real defects we report AU-sPRO \cite{bergmann2022}. Each annotated region counts as found once a defect-specific area is covered, false positives are pixels outside every region pooled over good and anomalous images, and the curve is integrated up to a 5\% false-positive rate. We re-implemented these rules at the $256{\times}256$ map resolution after reading the official evaluation code. On four pushpin heads, the unmodified official code applied to full-resolution copies of the maps agrees with our values to within 0.7 points. For real defects at fixed operating points, a compartment is a target when the annotation covers at least 20 of its pixels; the scene cutoff, threshold and list length are those fixed on the development trays. We write a paired difference as ``$+x$ [$a$, $b$]'': $x$ is the difference between two heads and $[a,b]$ its 95\% bootstrap interval. Intervals resample original photographs together with all their edits (matched trays, 4{,}000 draws) or test images (real defects, 2{,}000 draws), with every head evaluated on the same draws.
