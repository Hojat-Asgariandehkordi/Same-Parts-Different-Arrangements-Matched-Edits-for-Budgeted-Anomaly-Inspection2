\section{Inspection protocol}
\label{sec:supp_protocol}
The inspection protocol, comprising the cutoffs, the endpoints and the decision criteria, was specified before any test score was examined. The upstream-only continuation (\cref{sec:upstream_supp}) was added before test scoring, after training data showed that the class-wide rule-aware heads did not meet the compartment-ranking criterion, and the eligibility rules and edits of the juice experiment were fixed before any juice photograph was scored. All policies, seeds and interfaces are reported.

\paragraph{Scene and compartment cutoffs.}
The scene cutoff is the 63rd smallest maximum among 69 normal validation maps, and an alarm requires a strictly greater score. The compartment cutoff at recall $r$ is the $\lceil 138r\rceil$-th largest training target score among scenes that raise an alarm; both the vacated and the recipient compartment are included, even when one of them is difficult to localize, and the comparison is inclusive. If fewer than $\lceil 138r\rceil$ targets remain after the scene alarm, the cutoff is set to zero and the operating point is reported as infeasible. The fixed-cost cutoff is the $(\lfloor0.5\,n_{\rm dev}\rfloor+1)$-th largest score among the untouched compartments of training faults that raise an alarm, with a strict comparison, which admits at most 0.5 untouched compartments per training fault; it is likewise set to zero if too few negatives remain. Any compartment queued on a source or a sham counts as a false mark, and for the vacancy-only variant the vacated compartment is the only positive. Rank statistics are computed on the raw maps before the scene alarm, with exact ties broken by compartment index.

\paragraph{Decision criteria.}
The primary endpoint is the difference in untouched compartments queued per extracted fault between the footprint and the class-wide policy. A difference is considered useful if it removes at least one compartment with a 95\% interval that excludes zero, while the achieved target recall remains at least 90\% and declines by at most five points, the fault and vacancy alarm rates remain at least 95\% and decline by at most three points, and valid-sham alarms increase by at most five points. A mechanistic interpretation further requires that the class-wide control reach a training alarm rate of at least 90\% for both fault types, a fault-over-sham win rate of at least 90\% and a compartment AUROC of at least 90\%. Explaining the behaviour of the released model additionally requires that the class-wide control produce broad changes in untouched compartments, exceeding those of the vacancy and the sham. The seed replicates and the two photo halves are required to preserve the sign of the primary contrast. All intervals are conditional on the fitted heads and cutoffs.

\paragraph{Region area and diagnostic response.}
The region area uses binary saturation: each compartment is counted once, when its score crosses a common threshold. Positives are the two target compartments of each fault; negatives are its thirteen untouched compartments together with the 30 compartments of the corresponding source and sham, or only the 13 untouched compartments for the fault-only variant. Recall is interpolated linearly through ties and integrated up to a pooled false-compartment rate of 0.05, and the area is divided by 0.05. Both curves are descriptive sweeps over the test set, whereas the scene and compartment cutoffs are fixed on training data; bootstrap replicates resample complete photo groups and recompute the pooled sweep. In contrast to the official pixel-level AU-sPRO, which allows area-based saturation inside ambiguous native regions~\cite{bergmann2022}, our compartments carry an exact label and constitute indivisible units of inspection.

The any-pixel summary counts a compartment as changed whether it contains a single large change or a broad one. The complementary area fraction counts pixels whose absolute change is at least 0.5 on the same 13 compartments, weighted by compartment area within each original photograph, and we additionally report per-compartment upper quantiles averaged over compartments. These comparisons with the source serve diagnosis only and do not enter the inspection decision.
\section{Queue definitions for pooled trays and real defects}
\label{sec:supp_queue}
\label{sec:supp_defs}
\paragraph{Scene gate.} The scene cutoff is the 63rd smallest map maximum of the 69 unedited training trays (the split-conformal $\lceil 70\times0.9\rceil$ order statistic), and a tray alarms when its maximum is strictly greater. Under exchangeability this bounds the marginal false-alarm probability of a new normal tray by 10\%; it does not bound the realized rate on a particular test set.
\paragraph{Queues.} Compartments are the 15 cells of the tray grid, and a compartment's score is the maximum of the map inside it. Only an alarmed tray opens a queue. The threshold is the highest cutoff at which at least 95\% of training targets on alarmed trays are queued; the list length is the smallest $k$ with the same property (ties broken by compartment index); the relative cutoff is the largest $\alpha$ on a 0.001 grid with the same property. Pooled trays contain the 69 training relocations and the 69 training vacancies, and every parameter is refit on them. On real defects we use the pooled parameters of each head, because real trays have one or several faulty compartments.
\paragraph{Diagnostics.} Change statistics compare an edited map with its own source on the 13 compartments untouched by the relocation, for every edit, so the three edits are compared on identical cells. The cell analogue of low-FPR sPRO integrates target-compartment recall against the false-compartment rate up to 5\% and normalizes; ``fault'' uses the 13 untouched compartments of each faulty tray as negatives and ``all'' adds every compartment of the unedited source and the valid sham.
\section{Matched edits and the part-map extractor}
\label{sec:supp_photo}
We use the official MVTec LOCO photographs and fix the 372 good training, 69 good validation and 138 good test photographs before any experiment. The training photographs are split pseudo-randomly into 302 for fitting and 70 held out for model selection, and the test photographs into two fixed halves of 63 and 75. No test score or activation enters candidate selection. The authors' released maps determine the five-column, three-row tray geometry. On the 256-square grid we use $x=(8,57,105,153,201,249)$ and $y=(13,91,166,241)$. Retained pin labels are eight-connected class-2 components of at least 75 pixels. A qualifying source has one per cell. This filter is specific to the compartment experiment.

The RGB cutout is the pin-class component with the neighboring bright pixels from a two-pixel halo, with enclosed holes filled. Selection requires cell clearance and an available local dark background. We fill the original position with the per-channel median of the local dark class-4 ring. Destinations have a one-pixel cell margin, zero overlap with the source cutout or other pins, and at least a two-pixel pin clearance. The oracle component moves entirely onto class 4. The two translations have the same integer squared displacement and equal paste area. The displacement range and intensity thresholds were fixed on validation photographs. In test the moves range from 19.2 to 51.4 pixels. All 138 candidate images meet the edit rules. Sham and fault change exactly the same number of decoded RGB pixels in each pair. Source-pixel repaint is exact. All test masks retain the same class histogram for source, sham and fault. Each move preserves the pin-class component count. All four RGB arms use original camera backgrounds. The held-out scenes contain no generated textures or objects.

The RGB-only extractor is a two-level U-Net with widths 16, 32 and 64, SiLU, group normalization, max pooling and nearest-neighbor upsampling. It predicts six channels. The training label is the released map for each train/good photograph. Weighting is inverse square-root class frequency with floor 0.01. AdamW runs with seed 74817, learning rate $0.001$, decay $0.001$, batch size 12, and at most 30 epochs, selecting the lowest weighted loss on the 70 held-out images. The selected epoch is 30. Training pin IoU is 97.3\% against the released labels and all 70 training images have the correct retained cell counts. On held-out fault maps, pin IoU against the intervened oracle maps is 97.2\%. We keep extraction discrepancies in the test denominator. The extracted arm has its own validation cutoff. Its comparison with the oracle arm includes the effect of re-segmentation and recalibration.


\paragraph{Reference regions and scene operation.}
The two affected compartments are a rule-based coarse spatial reference. The vacant and occupied compartments need no pixel-level anomaly outline. For each cell we take the maximum of either the edited output or the absolute change from source. Even the change-based cell rank is 98.4\% on oracle maps while 90.3\% of untouched cells have a large absolute change. The matched deletion, which leaves the same vacancy, keeps changes above $0.5$ to 2.9\% of those same untouched cells. The paired difference is 87.4 points [82.4, 91.9] for oracle maps and 86.6 [81.4, 91.2] after extraction. The full threshold curve is Fig.~4 of the main paper. These are changes in the spatial output, whereas scene-level maxima and cutoff decisions are a separate measurement.

The image score is the official maximum sigmoid output. The validation IQRs are 0.00974 (oracle) and 0.01060 (extracted). The raw cutoffs are 0.15802 and 0.17097. The rule comparator sums the absolute deviation from one pin per compartment and adds retained out-of-grid pins. Its cutoff is zero on both paths. The comparator uses the same labels and the same validation rule. On re-extracted maps it yields zero sham alarms (exact 95\% upper bound 2.6\%), while retaining all 138 fault alarms. \Cref{tab:halves} reports the held-out halves and the paired effect. Figure selection uses the case closest to the median oracle fault-minus-sham score. There were zero missed faults. The second row uses the first hash-ordered alarmed sham, whose source also alarms.

\paragraph{Target construction.}
We build on the released SALAD implementation and checkpoints~\cite{fucka2025}. When a part is inserted, the released augmentation writes the donor footprint into the edited six-channel map and returns the entire resulting class plane as the discriminative target; the combined edit takes the union of this insertion mask and the same-image relabelling mask. The reconstruction objective uses the clean source labels with multiclass focal and Dice losses and is unaffected by these targets. The discriminative objective is
\[
  5\,{\rm mean}_{p}\,{\rm FL}_{\alpha,\gamma}(z_p,y_p)
  + {\rm mean}_{p}\,|\sigma(z_p)-y_p|,
\]
with the default sigmoid focal-loss parameters $\alpha=0.25$ and $\gamma=2$. Both terms average uniformly over all pixels of a batch, without area-dependent reweighting. The footprint policy records the paste mask before it is expanded to the class plane and modifies only the insertion part of the target, leaving the relabelling target of combined operations intact. Both policies share the Perlin perturbation, the bilinear rotation and the donor insertion and relabelling functions of the released code. Since the autoencoder is frozen, the reconstruction objective does not update either U-Net.

\paragraph{Training schedule.}
Training uses 372 normal training photographs and 371 eligible rule-aware training edits. Each batch of four contains two clean maps, one SALAD-style synthetic edit and one rule-aware edit, drawn as a relocation, a sham or a vacancy with probabilities $1/2$, $1/4$ and $1/4$; the four SALAD edit families are sampled uniformly. In the upstream-only continuation, the rule-aware slot is replaced by its clean source while all other slots, donors and transformations are kept, which preserves the 3:1 ratio of clean to synthetic examples of the original loader. We use Adam with weight decay $10^{-5}$ and an initial learning rate of $3{\times}10^{-5}$ for rule-aware fine-tuning and $10^{-5}$ for the continuation, train for 800 steps with batches of four, and decay the learning rate by a factor of 10 after step 760. The final checkpoints of seeds~1 and~2 are evaluated. Within each pair of runs, the initial checkpoint and the sequence of input batches are identical and only the targets differ, which we verified for every example. At inference, the fine-tuned head replaces the released head of identical architecture.

\section{Identical-input continuations (1.6k steps)}
\label{sec:supp_cont}
\label{sec:supp_ft}
For each category and seed, we load the released composition weights, freezes the released reconstruction autoencoder, and copies the discriminative U-Net into a class-wide and a footprint arm. Both arms consume one prefetched stream of 1{,}600 SALAD steps. Each step takes the next map of a per-epoch permutation of the official good training maps and its clean copy, and with probability 1/2 applies one of the four released edit families with a uniformly drawn donor map. The loss is SALAD's $5\times$ sigmoid focal plus L1 on the discriminative map. Adam ($10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$) runs with a tenfold decay at step 1{,}520, and the final weights are used. This matches the 800 synthetic edits and 3:1 clean-to-edited ratio of the 800-step fine-tunes of \cref{sec:supp_ft800}. The arms differ only in the insertion target, and we verify that both receive the same input sequence. In the released generator, an insertion's target covers a median of 16 times the pasted area (median of per-edit ratios over 204 replayed pushpin insertions; the medians of the two areas themselves are 19{,}800 and 416 pixels).
\section{Training from initialization (70k steps)}
\label{sec:supp_scratch}
\paragraph{Recipe.} We reproduce the composition part of the released SALAD training procedure. The autoencoder and discriminative U-Net are the released architectures, initialized from scratch with the seed. Each step takes the next map of a per-epoch random permutation of the official good training maps (372 for pushpins, 360 for screw bag) and a uniformly drawn donor map. With probability 1/2 one of the four released edit families is applied: Perlin-shaped relabeling, same-image component relabeling, rotated insertion of a donor component, or relabeling plus insertion. The batch is this map and its clean copy. The autoencoder reconstructs the clean map from the (possibly edited) input with the released multiclass focal loss ($\gamma=2$, released class weights) plus the released Dice loss. The U-Net receives the one-hot input and the reconstruction and is trained with $5\times$ mean sigmoid focal loss plus mean L1 against the discriminative target. Both networks use Adam at $10^{-5}$ with weight decay $10^{-5}$ and a tenfold decay at step 66{,}500 of 70{,}000. The appearance branches of SALAD are trained jointly in the original code but share no parameters or targets with the composition branch, so training the composition branch alone leaves its gradients unchanged up to the random stream. The Perlin-shaped edit uses a standard image rotation in place of the original augmentation library, as in the 800-step fine-tunes (\cref{sec:supp_ft800}).
\paragraph{The only difference between arms.} The released insertion edit pastes a rotated donor component and returns the entire channel of its class after pasting as the target. The \emph{class-wide} arm uses that target, as released. The \emph{footprint} arm uses the pasted donor mask captured before the expansion. For relabel-plus-insertion the relabel mask is united with either insertion target. Perlin and relabel targets are identical in both arms.
\paragraph{Identical inputs.} For each seed, the two networks are initialized once, copied to both arms, and both arms receive the same stream of training maps and edits. We record checksums of the full sequence of input maps, clean maps and both target sequences, and verify that the two arms of a seed receive identical inputs.
\paragraph{Scoring.} The final models (step 70{,}000) are scored on the oracle and re-extracted maps of every training and test edit, on the official native test maps, and in the insertion probe. Scene and spatial cutoffs are fit on training before test maps are read, exactly as for every other head.
\paragraph{Pre-specified endpoints.} The protocol fixed the primary endpoint before any from-initialization head was scored. That endpoint is footprint minus class-wide wasted inspections at the 95\% training threshold on the re-segmented path, with a useful effect defined as at least one compartment fewer and an interval excluding zero. It also fixed guardrails on scene recall, achieved target recall and valid-sham alarms, and a reproduction check of the released behaviour by the class-wide arm. \scratchEndpoints

\section{Instance-insertion probe}
\label{sec:supp_insertion}
For a category with a multi-instance part class we take every official validation (training) and test good part map and paste one intact instance of that class from an official training map. Donor instances are connected components of the class with an area in the interquartile range of training instances (pushpins, screw bag) or between 2{,}000 and 4{,}500 pixels (a single fruit in breakfast box, whose fruits usually merge into one region). The placement is drawn uniformly at random, with a fixed seed per image, among positions where the whole donor lies on the substrate on which the class normally rests (compartment floor, bag, or the cereal side of the box), at least three pixels from existing instances of the class. Pushpins then have an extra pin in a compartment, screw bags an extra part, and breakfast boxes a fruit in the wrong compartment; all three violate the published rules. ``Same-class any'' is the fraction of untouched same-class instances whose map changes by at least 0.5 somewhere, ``same-class area'' the fraction of their pixels that do, and ``other classes'' the same fraction over pixels of the remaining part classes more than ten pixels from the paste. Splicing connectors were excluded because their contacts leave no free substrate for an extra instance.

\paragraph{Rules.} We read the official MVTec LOCO evaluation code to fix three rules. (i) Each ground-truth file is one defect channel with a defect-specific saturation: $\min(\text{threshold},\text{area})$ pixels for absolute and $\text{threshold}\times\text{area}$ for relative saturation; the channel's sPRO at a threshold is $\min(\text{TP area}/\text{saturation},1)$. (ii) A false positive is a positive pixel outside every channel of its image, and the false-positive rate pools all good and anomalous test images. (iii) The area under the mean sPRO curve is integrated up to a maximum false-positive rate and divided by it. We apply these rules to the $256{\times}256$ maps: ground-truth channels are downsampled by nearest neighbour and absolute saturation thresholds are scaled by the area ratio. Thresholds are 3{,}001 quantiles of the negative pixels between the 85th and 100th percentile plus $\pm\infty$. Only logical anomalies and good images enter.

\section{Native LOCO and screw-bag analysis}
\label{sec:supp_loco}
The two released SALAD models of each category are used. We verified that the released models, the part-map decoder and the composition branch are used unmodified. The maps are indexed labels, mapped to six channels without RGB conversion or interpolation. The expected output is a finite $256\times256$ array in $[0,1]$. In each category the same validation map repeated exactly. The code normalizes the maximum using its normal validation mean and standard deviation. A positive affine transform preserves all rankings and the corresponding validation-order-statistic threshold. The released inputs contain 1,778 fitting, 305 normal validation and 1,568 test entries. The original benchmark paper reports 1,772, 304 and 1,568. SALAD fuses three branches: a local RGB appearance map, the composition map studied in the main paper, and a global RGB score that pools student features over the scene and over regions defined by the part map~\cite{fucka2025}. Our replay of the complete released system reproduces the published 100.0\% logical AUROC on 91 native logical pushpin faults and 138 normal photographs.

On the matched pushpins, however, the shams reveal a cost that this AUROC does not capture (\cref{fig:branches}b). Fusion ranks every fault above its sham but raises an alarm on 130 of 138 extracted shams and 75 of 138 sources, with 55 shams alarming although their source does not. The composition branch alarms on 7 shams, and an explicit one-pin-per-compartment rule applied to the same extracted maps raises no sham alarm while detecting every fault.

A second rule shows that the informative branch depends on the rule. MVTec LOCO requires the icon on the upper label of a juice bottle to match its liquid~\cite{bergmann2022}. The local RGB branch ranks the upper label first among five bottle regions for 74.2\% of faults but also for 85.4\% of shams, and alarms on 25 faults and 28 shams. The two rules therefore favour different branches: composition evidence decides placement in a tray, whereas global appearance captures a label--liquid correspondence that the part map cannot express.
\section{Response evidence in part graphs}
\label{sec:graph}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{graph_audit.pdf}
\caption{A masked GNN responds to switched links through node degree (78 GlaS fields, 12 donors). (a)~Edited-graph AUROC; local spacing adds 15.3 points to typed distance. (b)~AUROC of the change between matched graphs; hollow: 50 by construction. (c)~Top-four recovery of the masked GNN as fitted, with degree inputs fixed, and refit with mean aggregation. Donor-paired 95\% intervals.}
\label{fig:graph}
\end{figure}
The matched design also applies to graphs of parts. We build gland graphs from GlaS histology images~\cite{glas2017} and cardiac structure graphs from HVSMR-2.0~\cite{pace2024}, and apply \emph{degree-preserving switches} that remove two links and add two others so that every node keeps its degree. Readouts are evaluated by how well they rank the four switched pairs. On GlaS, adding local gland spacing to typed centroid distance improves the edited-graph AUROC by 15.3 points [10.9, 20.1], and the gain persists for additional length-matched switches and under joint matching of raw and local distances ($+16.8$ [11.3, 23.0]); these tiers reuse donors and involve no pathological labels.

For a candidate link $e=(u,v)$ between nodes $u$ and $v$, let $z_e$ be the predicted logit and $a_e\in\{0,1\}$ the observed link state. For a link predictor, the Bernoulli loss $\operatorname{softplus}(z_e)-a_ez_e$ changes with $a_e$ even when the evidence $z_e$ is fixed. A pair MLP observes only geometry and roles, so its probability cannot change and it ranks by response at chance level (\cref{fig:graph}b). A query-masked neighbourhood model has an additional route: removing the queried link before computing the degree $d_u$ of an endpoint yields $d_u-a_e$, so a switch changes the masked degree at the four target pairs although both graphs share the same degree sequence, an effect related to the target-link exclusion studied in~\cite{zhu2024spot}. Ranked by absolute change in probability, the masked GNN recovers 87.5\% of the switched pairs among its top four (\cref{fig:graph}c). Holding its degree inputs fixed reduces this to 33.7\%, and refitting with mean neighbourhood aggregation and no degree inputs on the same four GlaS folds, which reaches 95.7--98.2\% training AUROC on normal links, reduces it to 46.6\%, 40.9 points lower [32.8, 49.8]. Since mean aggregation still admits indirect cues such as an empty neighbourhood, both interventions indicate a degree pathway, and recovery of known graph edits should not be interpreted as evidence of validity.

\paragraph{Paired estimands.}

Face contact of at least $5\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ defines a link \cite{pace2024}. Part features include log area or volume, elongation, compactness and channel intensity moments.

\section{Graph scorers and readout controls}
\paragraph{Fitting.}
Four test folds use the original donor allocation. For each family and fold we order the fitting donors pseudo-randomly and take the first quarter, with a minimum of two, for training. The rest fit the two learned link models. Both use donor-balanced BCE, with the per-pair weight inversely proportional to the donor's field count and that field's candidate count. AdamW uses weight decay $0.002$ and batch size 512. Rates $0.002$ and $0.0007$ are compared using the smallest weighted training loss. We train up to 80 epochs with patience 12 and initial seeds 904--907. For GlaS there are nine fit and three training donors per fold. For HVSMR there are 34 and 11. Frozen logistic comparators retain all outer fitting donors and use the original $0.015$ regularization and feature scales.

\paragraph{Frozen references.}
Typed frequency uses add-one smoothing for the binary outcome within each unordered role pair. The typed centroid logit uses role-pair indicators, the log ratio of distance to the graph median distance, and the squared log-distance term. The log ratio is clipped to $[-5,3]$. Context adds the log local-spacing ratio, $\log(1+\mathrm{CN})$, Jaccard overlap, and the logs of one plus the smaller and larger masked degrees. Spacing and topology-only references select the corresponding context features. Feature scales are the fitting standard deviations floored at 0.35. The robust geometry reference uses rolewise medians and $1.4826$ times median absolute deviation floored at 0.15. The three shape descriptors of the part reference have a floor of 0.08 and the remaining intensity descriptors a floor of 0.025. The pooled fit is the fallback for unobserved types. All fits and fold assignments are in the reproduction scripts.

\paragraph{Input and capacity.}
The pair design is the sum of the endpoint one-hot roles, log distance normalized by the median pair distance, and log distance divided by the geometric mean of the two endpoints' local radii. The radii depend only on centroids. Both geometric logs are clipped to $[-5,3]$ in the learned models. The pair-only MLP has hidden widths 60, 60 and 12 with SiLU activations and 5,065 parameters. For the GNN, we copy the adjacency per query, set both directions of the query to zero, and build the two directed neighborhoods from the masked adjacency. For each neighbor the message has its role, both normalized distances to each endpoint, and a bit indicating incidence to the other endpoint. A $13\to32\to24$ SiLU network encodes the messages. For each endpoint we sum messages and divide by $\sqrt{1+\tilde d_i}$. We use the componentwise mean and maximum across endpoints plus the sorted $\log(1+\tilde d_i)$ values. The $60\to48\to24\to1$ head includes the same pair inputs, for 5,369 parameters total. No descriptors, adjacency, or embeddings other than the specified pair inputs enter the MLP. GNN training link AUROC spans 97.4--99.1\% on the original gland graph and 85.1--91.6\% on cardiac. Both models beat the fitting prevalence intercept in all eight original-family folds. The pair MLP meets the 0.80 training AUROC check in all four GlaS and three of four cardiac folds.

\paragraph{Repeatability and held-fixed evidence.}
We checked an observed link and a non-link on every scored source construction. Reversing the bit for the query alone gives identical inputs and an identical conditional probability in 680/680 checks. Repeated scores on the same input had zero drift. Reindexing vertices changes the learned probability by at most $4.77\times10^{-7}$. We use $2\times10^{-6}$ for float32 and $10^{-10}$ for the original float64 score family, consistent with the original similarity and reindexing controls. Numerical ties are distinct from changes of at least $0.10$ training IQR. The IQR is measured on unedited held-out graphs, with a $10^{-3}$ floor. A very small IQR for the observed-state loss can occur when most source non-links are assigned high confidence.

For the GNN, the two paths through observed-state bit $a$ and logit $z$ give direct term $D=-(a'-a)(z+z')/2$ and context term $C=\Delta s-D$. We report $\sum_T|D|/(\sum_T|D|+\sum_T|C|)$ per edit, then aggregate by source and donor. The mean on GlaS is 65.4\% [64.0, 67.2] and on HVSMR 71.1\% [68.1, 74.0]. Some changes cancel between terms. The context-frozen model holds $z$ fixed and reads $s(a',z)$. In GlaS its $|\Delta s|$ ranks all targets first, because its observed query bit is the only changing input. A high response of the loss can therefore differ from a high response of the fitted evidence.

The $31.47\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ calibration threshold is below the largest negative ($48.45$) and smallest positive ($58.17$). Axial-mask profiles detect 21/24 with zero negative alarms. Refitting without the degree terms raises AUROC to 44.2\%. Removing the shared-neighbor terms gives 18.3\%. Distance alone gives 75.4\%. Removing one incident link at a time while holding the SVC--PA bit fixed yields mean absolute within-patient probability shifts of 0.093 on the 21 Glenn-coded patients with at least one such link and 0.144 on ten code-negative patients. These are exploratory sensitivity checks on five fit patients and a single query. They do not resolve the causal effects of surgery. Five target-negative SVC variants occur in the release, with two in fit and three in calibration. The test has zero. Independent adjudication of native relations and benign variants is needed to extend the scope beyond this code.
\section{Data use}
The medical experiments use existing de-identified research releases and their original labels, and constitute a secondary analysis without new ethics review, participant recruitment, intervention or consent collection. The HVSMR authors report approval by the Boston Children's Hospital IRB with a waiver of written consent~\cite{pace2024}. The photographs derive from the public MVTec LOCO AD dataset, distributed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and derived image panels retain this attribution and license. Selected pairs, data splits, scores, calibration sets, checkpoint hashes and figure inputs are released with the code.
