\section{Order of analyses and what was registered}
\label{sec:supp_order}
The 138 test and 69 development trays, their edits, the RGB re-extractor and the released-map results were fixed in earlier rounds (Part B). The inspection protocol of the main paper builds on them as follows.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \emph{Registered before scoring.} A protocol (SHA-256 prefix \texttt{a1e0af83}) frozen before any new head was scored specifies the from-initialization replication of the label comparison with SALAD's full 70{,}000-step recipe (\cref{sec:supp_scratch}), its endpoints and guardrails, the pooled-tray analysis and the full-system calibration diagnostic. The earlier fine-tunes of Part B had their own locks.
\item \emph{Descriptive follow-ups of saved maps.} Threshold, list and relative queues for the released and earlier fine-tuned heads were computed on saved maps before that protocol was written.
\item \emph{Added during this round, not registered.} The identical-input continuations of the main paper (\cref{sec:supp_ft}), the native AU-sPRO evaluation (\cref{sec:supp_native}), the instance-insertion probe (\cref{sec:supp_insertion}) and the two appearance detectors (\cref{sec:supp_appearance}). They use the same fixed development/test split and decision rules, and every head and seed that was trained is reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Inspection definitions}
\label{sec:supp_defs}
\paragraph{Scene gate.} The scene cutoff is the 63rd smallest map maximum of the 69 unedited development trays (the split-conformal $\lceil 70\times0.9\rceil$ order statistic), and a tray alarms when its maximum is strictly greater. Under exchangeability this gives a marginal false-alarm probability of at most 10\% for a new normal tray; it does not bound the realized rate on any particular test set.
\paragraph{Queues.} Compartments are the 15 cells of the registered tray grid. A compartment's score is the maximum of the map inside it. Only an alarmed tray opens a queue. \emph{Threshold}: the highest cutoff $t$ such that at least 95\% of development targets on alarmed trays satisfy $g_c\ge t$. \emph{List}: the smallest $k$ such that the top $k$ compartments of alarmed development trays contain at least 95\% of targets (ties broken by compartment index). \emph{Relative}: the largest $\alpha$ on a 0.001 grid such that compartments with $g_c\ge\alpha\max g$ cover 95\% of development targets. For pooled trays the development set contains the 69 relocations and the 69 vacancy-only trays, and every parameter is refit on it.
\paragraph{Outcomes.} Target recall pools targets over trays. Wasted inspections $W$ count untouched compartments in the queue per anomalous tray. Clean-tray queues count all queued compartments on unedited sources and on valid shams, which are normal under the rule. A tray whose scene does not alarm contributes misses and no queue.
\paragraph{Diagnostics.} Change statistics compare an edited map with its own source on the 13 compartments untouched by the relocation, for every edit, so the three edits are compared on identical cells. ``Any'' is the fraction of those compartments with at least one pixel changing by 0.5 or more; ``area'' the fraction of their pixels that do. The region area is a cell analogue of low-FPR sPRO: target-compartment recall against the false-compartment rate, integrated up to 5\% and normalized. ``Fault-only'' uses the 13 untouched compartments of each faulty tray as negatives. ``All'' adds every compartment of the unedited source and of the valid sham.
\paragraph{Intervals.} Percentile intervals from 4{,}000 bootstrap resamples of original photographs. Every edit, input path and head of a photograph moves together, so contrasts between heads are paired. Recall intervals resample the pooled numerator and denominator.

\section{Identical-input continuations}
\label{sec:supp_ft}
\paragraph{Recipe.} For each category and seed, one process loads the released composition weights, freezes the released reconstruction autoencoder, and copies the discriminative U-Net into a class-wide and a footprint arm. Both arms consume one prefetched stream of 1{,}600 SALAD steps: the next map of a per-epoch permutation of the official good training maps and its clean copy; with probability 1/2 one of the four pinned edit families (Perlin relabeling, component relabeling, rotated donor insertion, relabeling plus insertion) with a uniformly drawn donor map. The loss is the pinned $5\times$ sigmoid focal plus L1 on the discriminative map. Adam ($10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$), tenfold decay at step 1{,}520, final weights. This matches the 800 synthetic edits and 3:1 clean-to-edited ratio of the earlier original-edit continuation (Part B). The arms differ only in the insertion target; the receipts store the hash of the shared input sequence (\texttt{results/finetune/*/fit.json}).
\paragraph{Scoring.} Every head is scored on the oracle and re-extracted maps of all development and test edits, on the official native test maps of its category (image AUROC from the map maximum; AU-sPRO, \cref{sec:supp_native}), and with the cutoffs of \cref{sec:supp_defs}.

\section{Native AU-sPRO}
\label{sec:supp_native}
We read the official MVTec LOCO evaluation code to fix three rules. (i) Each ground-truth file is one defect channel with a defect-specific saturation: $\min(\text{threshold}, \text{area})$ pixels for absolute and $\text{threshold}\times\text{area}$ for relative saturation, and its sPRO at a threshold is $\min(\text{TP area}/\text{saturation}, 1)$. (ii) A false positive is a positive pixel outside every channel of its image, and the false-positive rate pools all good and anomalous test images. (iii) The area under sPRO is integrated up to a maximum FPR (0.05 here) and divided by it. We apply these rules to the $256{\times}256$ maps. Each ground-truth channel is downsampled by nearest neighbour, and absolute saturation thresholds are scaled by the area ratio. Thresholds are 3{,}001 quantiles of the negative pixels between the 85th and 100th percentile, plus $\pm\infty$. Only logical anomalies and good images are used. The ground truth is fetched from the same pinned dataset mirror as the photographs, with file hashes in \texttt{inputs/loco\_gt/sha256.txt}.

\section{Instance-insertion probe}
\label{sec:supp_insertion}
For a category with a multi-instance part class we take every official validation (development) and test good part map, and paste one intact instance of that class from an official training map. Donor instances are connected components of the class with an area within the interquartile range of training instances (pushpins, screw bag) or between 2{,}000 and 4{,}500 pixels (a single fruit in breakfast box, whose fruits usually merge into one region). The placement is drawn uniformly among positions where the whole donor lies on the substrate on which the class normally rests (compartment floor; bag; the cereal side of the box) at least three pixels from existing instances of the class, using a hash of the file name. Pushpins then have an extra pin in a compartment, screw bags an extra screw, nut or washer, and breakfast boxes a fruit in the wrong compartment; all three violate the published rules. We score the composition branch on the source and edited maps. ``Same any'' is the fraction of untouched same-class instances whose map changes by at least 0.5 somewhere; ``same area'' the fraction of their pixels that do; ``other area'' the same fraction over pixels of the remaining part classes more than ten pixels from the paste. $W$ counts untouched same-class instances above the cutoff that reaches 95\% development recall of the pasted instance on alarmed maps.

\section{Appearance detectors}
\label{sec:supp_appearance}
\paragraph{EfficientAD.} SALAD's local appearance branch is an EfficientAD student--teacher--autoencoder \cite{batzner2024} with the released pushpin weights. We use the pinned normalization of the full-system run (Part B) and compute its normalized map on every development and test edit; the map depends on RGB only, so both input paths coincide.
\paragraph{PatchCore.} We implement PatchCore \cite{roth2022} with an ImageNet WideResNet-50-2, layer-2 and layer-3 features with $3{\times}3$ local averaging, layer-3 features upsampled to the layer-2 grid, a 1\% greedy coreset (3{,}809 patch features from the 372 official good training photographs, selected after a random projection to 128 dimensions), nearest-neighbour distances, bilinear upsampling to $256{\times}256$ and Gaussian smoothing with $\sigma=4$. Inputs are the same uncropped $256{\times}256$ composites as for the other detectors. Its native logical AUROC on the official pushpin test split is 73.2\%.
\paragraph{What the matched controls show.} Both detectors alarm on almost every valid sham (EfficientAD 128, PatchCore 123 of 138) while alarming on few unedited sources (9 and 15), and both queue the sham's edited compartment. Their maps locate where pixels were composited; on faulty trays this coincides with the targets, which is why fault-only localization favours them. The sham and source compartments in the ``all'' region area reverse the comparison with SALAD's composition map (main paper).

\section{Full SALAD operating point}
\label{sec:supp_full}
Full SALAD is replayed with the released weights and pinned code (Part B). The validation cutoff is the published operating point: per-branch normalization statistics come from the official validation split and the alarm cutoff is the split-conformal 10\% cutoff on the 69 unedited development trays. For the normal-test cutoff we fit the same rule on the unedited sources of one fixed half of the test trays and apply it to all edits of the other half, then swap halves and add the counts. The Gaussian branch's median normalized score is $+0.06$ on validation and $+2.73$ on unedited test trays on the extracted path (\cref{tab:system_supp}), with the same pattern on the oracle path; the other branches shift little. The shift is present in native good test images and does not depend on our edits.
