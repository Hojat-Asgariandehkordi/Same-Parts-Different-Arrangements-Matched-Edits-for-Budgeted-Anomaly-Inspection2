\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A pushpin tray in which one pin has slid into its neighbor's compartment contains only normal-looking parts. It is faulty because of where they are: one compartment is empty and another holds two pins. Logical anomaly detectors are built for exactly such cases \cite{bergmann2022,fucka2025,liu2023,kim2024psad}. On MVTec LOCO they are compared by image-level AUROC and by saturated per-region overlap (sPRO), a threshold-free summary of the anomaly map \cite{bergmann2022}. Neither number answers the question an inspector faces after an alarm: \emph{which compartments do I open, and how many of them are fine?}

This question is hard to study with natural test sets. Logical defects are rare, each is a different scene, and nothing is held fixed. A map that lights up the faulty compartments may be responding to the violation, to the part that was disturbed, or to an editing artifact. We therefore build \emph{matched rearrangements} of real photographs (\cref{fig:protocol}). The same intact pin is moved by the same distance, either within its compartment (a valid sham) or into an occupied neighbor (a fault). A vacancy-only control removes the pin and inserts nothing. Parts are re-segmented from every edited photograph, so the detector sees what it would see in deployment. All operating points are fixed on development trays before test maps are read, and the cost of an alarm is counted in the unit an inspector opens: a compartment.

Applied to SALAD \cite{fucka2025}, a state-of-the-art composition-based detector, the audit separates three properties that the usual metrics merge. Detection is perfect: all \relFaults{} faults alarm, every fault outscores its sham, and the two affected compartments rank at \relAUC\% AUROC. Ranking is good: both targets are among the top three compartments on \relTopThree\% of trays. Thresholding is poor: a cutoff calibrated for 95\% target recall opens \relThrW{} of the 13 intact compartments per faulty tray. The controls locate the cause. Inserting a pin changes \relAnyFault\% of the untouched compartments by at least 0.5. Removing it (\relAnyVac\%) or moving it validly (\relAnySham\%) does not. An insertion probe on other released checkpoints finds the same class-specific signature in screw bags, where one extra part raises \sbAny\% of the untouched parts of its class and no other parts.

This points at a deliberate design choice. SALAD labels a synthetic insertion over \emph{every} pixel of the inserted part's class, because an extra screw in an unordered bag cannot be told apart from the original screws \cite{fucka2025}. That label rewards the head for marking all instances of a class whenever one is added. We test the explanation by retraining the head twice per seed on bit-identical inputs, changing only the insertion label to the pasted footprint (\cref{fig:teaser}). The spread falls from \cwSpread\% of untouched compartments to \fpSpread\%. The fix also matters on real defects. On the 91 native logical pushpin anomalies of MVTec LOCO, footprint labels raise AU-sPRO at 5\% false-positive rate from \natPPcw\% to \natPPfp\%, and on extra-pin defects from \natAddCW\% to \natAddFP\%. This costs about 1.5 points of image AUROC relative to the same retraining with class-wide labels. In screw bags, where any of the identical parts could be the extra one and the benchmark annotates them all, footprint labels bring nothing (AU-sPRO \natSBfp\% against \natSBcw\%). The right spatial extent for a synthetic label is the one the inspection task uses to locate the fault.

The matched controls also expose what fault-only evaluation misses. Two appearance detectors, EfficientAD \cite{batzner2024} and PatchCore \cite{roth2022}, localize the faulty compartments better than SALAD's composition map when only faulty trays are scored. Yet they alarm on \eadSham{} and \pcSham{} of 138 valid shams: they find the edit, not the violation. SALAD's fused system reaches 100\% logical AUROC on native pushpins, yet its published cutoff alarms on \fullSrcVal{} of 138 normal test trays.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item A matched-rearrangement audit with same-part valid shams, vacancy-only controls and re-extracted inputs on real trays, together with an operating-point protocol that reports target recall and wasted inspections for threshold, list and relative queues fixed on development data (\cref{sec:method}).
\item A diagnosis of SALAD in which perfect detection and ranking coexist with a map that spreads to intact parts of the inserted class, localized by the controls and reproduced across categories (\cref{sec:diagnosis}).
\item Controlled evidence that the spatial extent of the synthetic insertion label causes the spread. Footprint labels, which every synthetic edit provides for free, more than double localization of native logical anomalies whose location is identifiable, and leave unchanged those the benchmark itself annotates as ambiguous (\cref{sec:cause,sec:native}).
\item Evidence that valid shams change conclusions that fault-only localization would draw about appearance detectors and SALAD's full system (\cref{sec:system}).
\end{itemize}
